# LW-133: Hyper-V crash campaign for the copy gate (G5)

Status: planned. Minted 2026-10-25 by the research programme (docs/51).
Milestone: M3. Priority: P0.
Dependencies: LW-041, LW-040.
Owner decision HUMAN_TODO q-47 Q2 (2026-10-25) chose Hyper-V for G5. Owner question Q1 (docs/51 section 9) is open: Hyper-V cmdlet access, through the "Hyper-V Administrators" group or one elevated shell.

## Scope
Run the G5 power-loss proof for the copy gate as a Hyper-V campaign: hard power-offs (`TurnOff`) of a Windows guest at every named crash point of the copy engine plus random times, checking the journal, staging and publication invariants after each (docs/51 section 4.1; docs/47 section 10). It builds on the C6 crash matrix (LW-041) and the copy-only transactions (LW-040), and runs from the campaign skeleton with its dry-run self-test (docs/51 section 8). The floor is 20 clean hard-offs and the target is 2,000, reported as a separate, higher label rather than as the gate (docs/51 section 3 item 3). The item also owns the 100,000-trace pure fault-injection model (docs/51 section 2.2). Hyper-V cannot prove that a USB bridge or HDD honours a flush when its own power goes; that claim belongs to LW-134. Lab data only: the VM VHDX sits on `E:` with disposable synthetic data, never on the owner's archive disk `H:`.

## Acceptance evidence
At least 20 clean hard-offs across every crash point with zero invariant violations is the G5 floor; 2,000 is the target, recorded as a higher label (docs/51 sections 4.1 and 5 P1). Each run checks the journal and the staging and publication invariants after recovery, and no run produces a false verified receipt. The 100,000-trace pure fault-injection model runs without a VM and records its trace count. The skeleton's dry-run self-test passes before any VM is built. Resource limits are recorded: about 4 to 8 GB RAM while the guest runs, and a VM VHDX of about 40 GB plus a 20 GB image on `E:` (docs/51 X1). The campaign starts only on the owner's go-ahead.
